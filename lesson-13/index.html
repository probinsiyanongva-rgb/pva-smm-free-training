QA, access, privacy, rights, and documentation — finishing work properly, not just making it look good.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

Professional SMM work is not finished when the content looks good. It is finished when the content is accurate, approved, correctly prepared, appropriately accessible, authorized, and documented.

Learn — 13.1 Four QA gates
GateQuestions
ContentIs it accurate, clear, on-brand, audience-relevant, and aligned with the objective?
TechnicalCorrect platform, account, asset, link, format, schedule, tags, and version?
RiskAny privacy, safety, rights, unsupported claims, or sensitive-topic issue?
WorkflowApproval recorded, status updated, evidence saved, follow-up assigned?
13.2 Security basics
  • Use approved account-access methods
  • Prefer delegated/role-based access when the platform supports it
  • Do not share credentials casually
  • Use least-privilege access — only what the role needs
  • Protect screenshots, exports, and customer information
  • Do not place confidential client/customer information into unapproved tools
  • Report suspected security incidents through the client's process
  • Do not hide mistakes or delete evidence of an incident without authorization
13.3 Content rights

Before using an image, video, music, testimonial, customer post, or other third-party asset, determine whether the client has permission or an appropriate license. "It is on the internet" is not the same as "the client has the right to use it."

Observe — final QA

Find every blocker

A fictional post package contains: a broken URL, an unapproved customer photo, an unsupported statistic ("#1 pantry brand in the region"), and a correct, approved caption.

Three blockers: the broken URL (Technical gate), the unapproved customer photo (Risk gate — rights/permission issue), and the unsupported statistic (Risk gate — unverifiable claim). The caption itself is fine, but the package as a whole isn't ready to publish until all three are resolved or removed.
Practice — access scenario

A shared spreadsheet full of passwords

A client asks the VA to use a shared spreadsheet containing social account passwords. Explain the safer approach, and what should be documented.

Ask if the platform supports delegated or role-based access instead (e.g. being added as an admin/collaborator on the actual account) rather than using a raw password from a spreadsheet. If that's genuinely not possible, flag the security risk to the client directly and ask them to at least limit who has access to that spreadsheet. Document that this was raised, what the client decided, and any access granted — so there's a record if something goes wrong later.
Check — rights scenario

"Feel free to use this!"

A customer tags the brand in a photo and says, "Feel free to use this!" List what should still be clarified before the client uses the image in marketing — if anything.

Even with an informal green light, it's worth confirming: does "use this" cover paid ads too, or just organic posts? Is it okay to crop/edit the photo? Can their name or handle be credited publicly? Is anyone else identifiable in the photo who'd also need to be comfortable with it? A quick DM confirming scope protects both the client and the customer, and gives the VA something to document.
