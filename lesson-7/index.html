Visual Content & Canva for SMM VAs

Basic visual communication and accessibility — not a design certification, just clear and on-brand.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Create simple, readable social content using approved brand direction
  • Apply visual hierarchy, spacing, contrast, and consistency
  • Understand the role of short-form video concepts and storyboards
  • Use accessibility checks where supported
  • Recognize when a visual-design task exceeds beginner SMM scope
The SMM VA does not need to become a professional designer. The goal is to produce or coordinate clear, on-brand assets that support the content objective.

Important: this is not a Canva mastery course. Keep the visual component practical and beginner-level — visual content is one part of SMM management, not the whole job. This lesson ends with your seventh work sample: the Basic SMM Content Set.

Learn — 7.1 Visual hierarchy

Make the most important information obvious. Use fewer competing elements, readable type, sufficient contrast, consistent spacing, and clear grouping.

7.2 Accessibility

Where the platform or design tool supports it, consider alt text, captions, readable contrast, typography, and text that does not rely on color alone. Canva currently provides accessibility checks for contrast, typography, and alt text, and supports captions for video/audio content.

7.3 Short-form video

At beginner level, focus on concept, structure, hook, visual sequence, on-screen text, spoken content/captions, CTA, and required assets. Don't turn this into advanced videography.

Observe — visual QA

A carousel with real problems

Picture a fictional carousel: tiny text over a busy background photo, low contrast (light gray text on white), six different fonts across four slides, dense paragraphs of text, and a CTA hidden at the very bottom of the last slide.

List the problems, and the first three fixes you'd make.

Problems: tiny text is hard to read at feed size; low contrast fails basic readability; six fonts break visual consistency and look unbranded; dense paragraphs discourage reading on a fast-scrolling feed; a buried CTA means most viewers never see it.

First three fixes, in priority order: (1) increase text size and contrast so it's readable at a glance — this affects whether the content works at all; (2) cut to one or two brand fonts across all slides; (3) break dense paragraphs into short phrases or bullet points, and move the CTA earlier or repeat it so it's not missed.
