Lesson 9 of 13

Community Management, Engagement & Social Listening

Monitoring, routine engagement, and listening — more than just replying.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Distinguish community engagement from social listening
  • Classify routine comments and messages
  • Write appropriate routine responses within client guidance
  • Identify recurring questions and useful audience signals
  • Recognize comments that require escalation
Community work is not only replying. It is paying attention to what people are saying, helping routine conversations move forward, documenting useful signals, and knowing when a conversation becomes a client decision.

This lesson ends with your ninth work sample: the Community & Listening Pack.

Learn — 9.1 Engagement vs. listening
ActivityExample
EngagementReplying to a customer's routine question
ModerationApplying the client's rules to spam or inappropriate content
ListeningCollecting recurring questions about a product
InsightNoticing that multiple people misunderstand the same feature
EscalationFlagging a safety allegation or serious complaint

Current 2026 platform commentary increasingly treats community participation, discovery, and audience signals as part of effective social work — active participation and comments-as-a-source-of-answers are common current signals. Treat these as current signals, not universal rules for every platform.

Check — listening log

15 interactions, worth paying attention to

  1. "Do you ship to Cebu?"
  2. "This gift box made my mom cry (happy tears!) 😭"
  3. "Is the adobo paste spicy?"
  4. "Do you ship to Cebu?" (different person)
  5. "I love that you explain where each product is from."
  6. "Can I get this without the coffee? I don't drink coffee."
  7. "Do you ship internationally?"
  8. "Following since your first post, love watching this grow."
  9. "Is the adobo paste spicy?" (different person)
  10. "Your packaging is so pretty, I want to keep the box!"
  11. "Can I customize what's inside the gift box?"
  12. "Is the adobo paste spicy?" (a third person)
  13. "I received a damaged jar and I'm honestly furious, this better be resolved."
  14. "Do you have a physical store?"
  15. "Do you ship to Cebu?" (a third person)

Extract: three recurring questions, two positive themes, one content opportunity, and one issue that should be escalated.

Recurring questions: shipping to Cebu, whether the adobo paste is spicy, whether the gift box can be customized.
Positive themes: the gift box as an emotional/gifting experience, appreciation for product origin stories.
Content opportunity: a post or FAQ addressing shipping coverage and spice level directly, since three separate people asked each.
Escalate: the damaged jar complaint — that's a product/service issue needing a real resolution, not something to handle with a generic reply.
