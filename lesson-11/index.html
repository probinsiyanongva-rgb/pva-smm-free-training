Social Media Analytics & Reporting

Metrics, interpretation, reporting, and improvement — turning numbers into decisions, not just summaries.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Explain common social media metrics in plain language
  • Distinguish activity metrics from business-relevant outcomes
  • Identify patterns without overclaiming causation
  • Write a concise monthly report
  • Turn observations into testable next steps
Reporting is not the act of copying numbers into a spreadsheet. The useful question is: What happened, what might it mean, what evidence supports that interpretation, and what should be tested next?

This lesson ends with your eleventh work sample: the Monthly Social Media Performance Report.

Learn — 11.1 Basic metrics
MetricPlain-language meaningCaution
ReachApproximate number of accounts exposed to contentNot the same as attention or business impact
ImpressionsTimes content was displayedCan include multiple displays to the same person
ViewsTimes video/content was viewed per platform's definitionDefinitions vary by platform
EngagementLikes, comments, shares, saves, depending on platformQuantity does not equal quality
ClicksRecorded clicks on a link or destinationCheck whether clicks led to meaningful action
Profile actionsActions taken on a profileInterpret in relation to the objective
Follower changeNet change in followersDoes not measure audience quality by itself
Leads/conversionsBusiness actions tracked by the client's systemRequires reliable attribution and context
11.2 Observation → Interpretation → Action

Example: "Educational carousel A received more saves than the other posts in the sample." → Interpretation: "The topic or format may have been useful enough to revisit." → Action: "Test two related educational posts and compare saves and downstream actions." This is more defensible than saying the carousel "caused growth."

Write a five-sentence executive summary

Check — vanity-metric trap

"Post B is our best because it reached the most people."

Write a respectful response explaining what additional evidence should be considered.

"Reach is a great sign that Post B got in front of a lot of people! To know if it's really our 'best' post, I'd also want to look at what people did after seeing it — saves, clicks, comments — since those tell us whether it actually moved people toward what we want. Post B's engagement was actually lower than a couple of our lower-reach posts, so it might be worth digging into why before we call it the top performer."
