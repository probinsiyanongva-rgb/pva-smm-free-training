Lesson 4 of 13

Content Pillars, Formats & Platform Fit

Content architecture and platform-native thinking — turning a strategy into a real content system.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Explain the difference between a content pillar and a content format
  • Create a balanced set of content pillars
  • Match content ideas to audience value and business goals
  • Adapt an idea to a platform rather than blindly duplicating it
  • Recognize when a trend is useful versus distracting
A content pillar describes what the brand consistently talks about. A format describes how an idea is delivered. Platform fit asks whether the idea makes sense in the environment where it will appear.

This lesson ends with your fourth work sample: the Content Pillar & Format Matrix for Amihan Kitchen.

Learn — 4.1 Content pillars

Useful beginner pillars can include education, product/service value, behind the scenes, customer stories, community, FAQs, brand perspective, and practical tips. The right pillars depend on the client.

4.2 Formats

Examples include static posts, carousels, short-form video, stories, polls, text-led posts, photo posts, and other formats supported by the chosen platform.

4.3 Platform fit

One idea can travel across platforms, but the treatment may change. Current platform guidance continues to emphasize original, relevant content, active community participation, discovery, and platform-specific experiences — for example, short-form video platforms have leaned toward curiosity, community, and search/discovery behavior, while other platforms have emphasized original content and creation/performance tools. These are useful current signals, not permanent rules — platform guidance shifts, and a client's actual platform behavior always matters more than a general trend.

Quick check — pillar or format?
Observe — one idea, three treatments

"How to store a regional pantry product after opening"

Describe how this one idea might become each of the following. There's no single right answer — think about what changes and what stays the same.

As a Facebook educational post
As an Instagram carousel
As a short-form video
Facebook post: A short, friendly text post with a clear tip — "Once opened, keep it in an airtight container out of direct sunlight and it'll stay good for weeks" — plus a product photo. Facebook audiences often read longer text comfortably.

Instagram carousel: Slide 1 hooks with "Just opened your [product]? Read this first," then 3–4 slides break the storage steps into simple visual cards, ending with a save-worthy recap slide.

Short-form video: A quick 15–20 second demo — show the product, show it being sealed and stored properly, on-screen text reinforcing the key point, ending on the product itself.

The underlying idea and the core tip don't change. The pacing, format, and how much is shown vs. told does.

Check — trend filter

A trending audio doesn't fit the brand

A trending audio is popular but does not fit Amihan Kitchen's warm, grounded, informative personality. Write the decision you would make, and the question you would ask before using it (if you'd ask one at all).

One reasonable approach: Skip the trend if it clashes with the brand's tone — chasing every trend at the cost of brand consistency isn't a good trade for a small, story-driven brand like this one. If there's real upside (genuine reach opportunity), the question to ask before using it is: "Can we adapt this trend's format or energy in a way that still sounds like Amihan Kitchen, or does it only work with the tone we'd have to imitate exactly?" If the answer is "only if we imitate it exactly," that's a sign to skip it.
