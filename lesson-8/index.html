Scheduling, Publishing & Approval Workflow

Execution accuracy and approval controls — the difference between "ready to schedule" and "ready to publish."

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Explain the difference between drafting, approval, scheduling, and publishing
  • Identify common pre-publishing checks
  • Track content readiness and approval
  • Verify published content after posting
  • Handle scheduling problems without hiding errors
A post being ready to schedule is different from a post being ready to publish. The VA should know the client's approval rule and verify the details before the content goes live.

This lesson ends with your eighth work sample: the Publishing & Approval Plan.

Learn — 8.1 Pre-publish checklist
  • Correct platform and account
  • Correct asset and version
  • Caption reviewed
  • Links tested
  • CTA correct
  • Required tags or mentions confirmed
  • Approval recorded
  • Date/time/time zone correct
  • Rights/permissions checked
  • Sensitive claims or information reviewed
8.2 After publishing

Verify that the correct post appeared, links work where relevant, formatting is acceptable, and the post is recorded as published. If something is wrong, document the issue and follow the client's correction/escalation process.

Observe — the "schedule this" request

A broken link, and an instruction to post anyway

The client sends an asset with a broken link and says, "Please schedule it now." Write the safest next step.

Don't schedule it as-is, and don't silently fix it either. Flag it back to the client first: "Before I schedule this, I noticed the link in the asset isn't working — can you confirm the correct link so I don't publish something broken?" Scheduling a known error just to follow an instruction quickly isn't actually helpful — a VA's job includes catching problems before they go live, not just executing requests literally.

Check — publishing error

A post goes live with the wrong image

Write a concise internal note using: Issue → Evidence → Impact → Action → Next Step.

Issue: Wrong product image published on the [date] Instagram post.
Evidence: Live post shows the gift-box image instead of the approved single-jar image; screenshot saved.
Impact: Post is live with an inaccurate product image; risk of customer confusion about what's actually being promoted.
Action: Replaced the image with the approved asset and re-published; logged the correction time.
Next Step: Confirmed with the client that the correction is visible; double-checking asset file names before scheduling going forward to prevent a repeat.
