Understand the Client, Brand & Audience

Client research, brand context, audience, and goals — the foundation everything else in this course builds on.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Extract useful information from a client brief
  • Separate facts, assumptions, and missing information
  • Describe a brand's audience in practical terms
  • Identify business and social media goals
  • Create clarification questions before content planning
Good SMM work starts with understanding. A content idea that looks creative can still be wrong if it does not fit the client, audience, brand, platform, or goal.

This lesson ends with your second work sample: the Brand & Audience Brief — built for this course's simulated client, Amihan Kitchen, who you'll work with again in later lessons and the Final Project.

Learn — 2.1 The client brief

What a useful beginner brief captures

A useful beginner brief captures the business, offer, audience, brand voice, current presence, goals, platforms, constraints, content resources, approval process, and known risks.

QuestionWhy it matters
What does the business sell?Prevents vague content.
Who is the audience?Changes examples, language, and content choices.
What is the business trying to accomplish?Determines what success means.
What should the brand sound like?Protects consistency.
Which platforms matter?Avoids creating for platforms the client does not use.
Who approves content?Prevents unauthorized publishing.
What claims or topics are restricted?Reduces brand and compliance risk.
2.2 Facts, assumptions, questions

A beginner should be comfortable writing: "The brief confirms X. I can reasonably infer Y, but I need the client to confirm it before publishing."

Observe — the incomplete brief

Reading between the lines of a short brief

The client says: "We sell Filipino pantry products to busy adults and want more engagement." The brief does not state the priority platform, approved tone, product claims, posting frequency, or whether customer photos may be reused.

List what's known, what's unknown, and what you'd ask before creating content.

Known (confirmed facts):
  • The business sells Filipino pantry products
  • The audience is described as "busy adults"
  • The client wants more engagement
Unknown (needs confirmation):
  • Which platform is the priority
  • The approved brand tone
  • What product claims are safe to make
  • How often the client wants to post
  • Whether customer photos may be reused
Questions to ask the client:
  • "Which platform matters most to you right now — Facebook, Instagram, or both equally?"
  • "Is there a tone or personality you want the page to sound like, or any language to avoid?"
  • "Are there any product claims — health, origin, exclusivity — we should NOT make?"
  • "If a customer shares a photo using your product, is it okay for us to repost it with credit?"

Check — assumption audit

Which parts of your profile are assumptions?

Review the audience profile you just wrote. For each field, decide: is this something the brief actually confirms, or is it your reasonable inference? List the statements that are assumptions, then turn the important ones into questions you'd ask the client.

Example: "Busy adults" is confirmed by the client's own words. But assuming they're specifically working professionals, or that they're shopping for themselves rather than for gifts, is an assumption — Amihan Kitchen's goals also mention gifting, so I'd ask: "When you say 'busy adults,' are you thinking mainly of people buying for themselves, people buying gifts, or both?" Any assumption that would change what you'd write or design is worth turning into a question.
