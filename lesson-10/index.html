Lesson 10 of 13

Comments, DMs, Reputation & Escalation

Sensitive communication and reputation handling — protecting the client comes before winning the conversation.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Recognize different levels of response risk
  • Write calm, concise routine replies
  • Know when a response needs client approval
  • Document and escalate sensitive issues
  • Avoid improvising on legal, safety, privacy, refund, or reputational matters
A reliable SMM VA does not try to win every public conversation. The goal is to protect the client, communicate appropriately, follow policy, and escalate matters that require a business decision.

This lesson ends with your tenth work sample: the Response & Escalation Pack.

LevelAction
RoutineRespond using approved information and tone

High-riskDo not improvise. Protect information, document facts, and follow the client's incident/escalation process
10.2 Examples of high-risk situations
  • Safety allegation
  • Privacy request
  • Threat or serious harassment
  • Potential fraud/scam
  • Legal threat
  • Sensitive customer information exposed publicly
  • Major factual claim about the business
  • Viral complaint with unclear facts
Quick check — which response level?
Observe — response boundary

"I will report you"

A commenter says: "Your product caused an allergic reaction. I will report you."

Write what the VA should do internally before writing any public reply.

This is a safety allegation — high-risk by definition. Before writing anything public: document exactly what was said (screenshot, timestamp), do not admit fault or diagnose anything, and immediately notify the client/authorized team member so they can decide how to handle it (which may involve their own safety, legal, or customer service process). A beginner VA's job here is to protect the client and the commenter by routing this correctly — not to write a reassuring reply on the spot.
Practice — escalation note

Write the escalation note for the allergic-reaction comment

Use Issue → Evidence → Impact → Action → Next Step.

Check — public response

Draft the neutral public acknowledgment

Write a reply to the allergic-reaction comment that does not admit fault, does not request sensitive information publicly, and does not make a promise the VA isn't authorized to make.

"Hi, thank you for letting us know, and we're sorry to hear this. We take this seriously — could you send us a DM with more details so our team can look into it directly?" This acknowledges the person without admitting fault, moves sensitive details to a private channel, and doesn't promise a specific resolution the VA isn't authorized to make.
