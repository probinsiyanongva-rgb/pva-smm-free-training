Lesson 6 of 13

Captions, Hooks & Calls-to-Action

Basic social writing — clear, honest, and audience-relevant, without needing to sound clever.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Write clear, audience-relevant hooks
  • Use a simple caption structure
  • Choose a CTA that matches the post objective
  • Edit generic or exaggerated copy into useful copy
  • Avoid unsupported claims and engagement bait as a default tactic
A good caption does not need to sound clever. It needs to make the value or context clear, fit the brand voice, and give the audience an appropriate next step.

This lesson ends with your sixth work sample: the Hook, Caption & CTA Pack for Amihan Kitchen.

Learn — 6.1 A practical structure

Hook → Context or value → Supporting detail → CTA, when useful. Not every caption needs every element.

6.2 Hook types
TypeWhat it does
ProblemIdentify a real audience problem
QuestionInvite reflection or curiosity
BenefitState a useful outcome
ObservationName something the audience recognizes
StoryBegin with a specific moment
CuriosityCreate a reason to continue without misleading the reader
6.3 CTA selection

Use a CTA that fits the purpose: comment with an experience, save a tip, share with someone who needs it, visit a page, ask a question, learn more, or take an approved business action.

Observe — caption diagnosis

What's wrong with this caption?

"🔥🔥 THE BEST PANTRY BRAND IN THE COUNTRY!! Comment your favorite flavor 👇 Tag a friend who needs this 🏷️ Share this post if you love Filipino food ❤️ Shop now, link in bio!!"

List the problems before rewriting it.

  • Unsupported claim: "best pantry brand in the country" — nothing in the brief supports this, and it's exactly the kind of statistic/claim the client brief says not to invent.
  • Three competing CTAs: comment, tag, AND share, AND shop now — the reader doesn't know which one actually matters.
  • Vague hype with no real content: there's no actual value, story, or information — just excitement with nothing underneath it.
  • Doesn't match brand voice: Amihan Kitchen's voice is warm, grounded, and informative — this reads as loud and generic instead.
