Build a Social Media Content Calendar

Planning, sequencing, status tracking, and operational coordination — turning ideas into a real production schedule.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Build a realistic four-week content calendar
  • Use content pillars to create variety without randomness
  • Include operational fields that support publishing
  • Track content status and approval
  • Balance consistency with realistic production capacity
A content calendar is an operating document. It should help the client and VA understand what is planned, why it exists, what is needed, who must approve it, and what stage it is in.

This lesson ends with your fifth work sample: the Four-Week Content Calendar for Amihan Kitchen.

Observe — calendar overload

28 posts, but only 12 can realistically get approved

A beginner plans 28 posts in one month even though the client can realistically approve 12. Explain why this is a workflow problem, not evidence of ambition.

More posts than the client can approve doesn't mean more gets published — it means a bottleneck. Drafts pile up waiting for review, the VA spends time creating content that may never go out, and the client feels rushed or overwhelmed instead of in control. A calendar sized to real approval capacity (here, 12) is more useful than a full one that mostly sits in "Awaiting Approval." Ambition should show up in the quality and variety of 12 posts, not in producing volume nobody can process.
