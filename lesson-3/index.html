Lesson 3 of 13

Social Media Strategy Fundamentals

Goals, audience, platform choice, content objectives, and metrics that actually mean something.

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Connect business goals to social media objectives
  • Distinguish awareness, engagement, traffic, leads, conversion, and retention objectives
  • Choose practical primary and secondary metrics
  • Explain why follower count alone is not a sufficient measure of success
  • Write a concise strategy snapshot
Strategy is a set of choices. It explains who the content is for, what it is intended to accomplish, where it will appear, what themes will support the goal, and how results will be evaluated.

This lesson ends with your third work sample: the Social Media Strategy Snapshot for Amihan Kitchen.

Learn — 3.1 From business goal to social goal
Business goalPossible social objectivePossible useful signals
Increase awarenessReach relevant peopleReach, qualified views, profile activity
Drive product interestHelp people evaluate an offerClicks, product-page visits, saves, questions
Build communityEncourage useful interactionMeaningful comments, replies, shares, recurring participation
Generate leadsMove interested people to an inquiry actionQualified inquiries, form starts/submissions
Improve retentionHelp existing customers stay informed and engagedRepeat interaction, saves, customer questions, retention actions where measurable
Metrics are context-dependent. A metric can be useful without being the goal itself. Always ask: "What decision would this number help us make?"
Quick check

Match each business goal to the objective that fits it best.

Observe — metric mismatch

20,000 views, and then nothing

A post receives 20,000 views but no meaningful clicks, saves, inquiries, or other relevant actions. Write three possible interpretations — and one thing you cannot conclude from views alone.

A few possibilities:
  • The hook or opening got attention, but the content itself didn't feel relevant enough to act on
  • The audience reached wasn't actually the intended audience — high reach, wrong people
  • The call-to-action was missing, unclear, or asked for too much (e.g. "shop now" with no easy next step)

What you can't conclude from views alone: that the content "failed," or that the audience isn't interested in the topic. Views tell you people saw it — they don't tell you why they didn't act, or whether the underlying idea was wrong versus just the execution.

Practice — objective selection

Choose a primary objective and two supporting metrics

For each scenario below, choose one primary objective and write two supporting metrics you'd track.

Check — strategy quality

Rewrite a vague strategy

A strategy says: "Post every day to get more followers." Rewrite it so it identifies an audience, purpose, content direction, and evidence of success.

One reasonable rewrite: "Post 3–4 times a week for adults interested in Filipino food and gifting, focused on helping them understand the products and feel connected to the stories behind them. Success is measured by saves, product-page clicks, and meaningful comments — not follower count alone."

Notice what changed: it names who it's for, why it exists, what the content is about, and how you'd know it's working.
